# Brief for whoever defends this at the pre-screening
### Self-contained. Assumes you have not read anything else. ~15 minutes.

---

## THE 30-SECOND VERSION

If you remember nothing else, remember this:

> **Nagpur's water is tested. By three different government bodies. The results are published — as one city-wide percentage, as scattered points in a 295-page PDF, and as 37 separate map files.**
>
> **So no resident of this city can answer "is my water safe?" about the place they actually live.**
>
> **We didn't measure anything new. We took what the government already publishes and connected it to the person it's about.**

Everything else is detail.

---

## 1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS

A map of every published water measurement in Nagpur — 395 of them — where you can click any point, or any empty spot, and get a straight answer about what is known and what isn't.

**And a rule we designed around: the system will never tell you your water is safe.** It reports three states only:

- **Exceeds** a named legal limit — with the parameter, the value, the limit, and what to do
- **No exceedance** among the parameters actually tested there
- **Not tested** — no measurement exists for this location

Why we refuse to say "safe": certifying drinking water under IS 10500 requires bacteriological testing. We have that for only a handful of points. **Claiming safety we can't prove would be the one thing that discredits everything else.**

## 2. THE PROBLEM — with the actual numbers

Learn these. They're all from government sources.

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| OCW publishes **99.6% of samples fit** — one figure for **3 million people**, no zone breakdown | OCW published data, to May 2026 |
| CGWB sampled **347 groundwater points** across the district. Median distance from the city centre: **35 km** | CGWB aquifer mapping |
| Inside the city, NEERI sampled **12 groundwater sources. All 12 showed faecal coliform.** IS 10500 requires zero | CSIR-NEERI for NMC, 2023-24 |
| **21 of 23 river sites fail** CPCB Class D — the minimum for fish to survive | Same |
| Dissolved oxygen down the Nag: **3.67 → 0.48 mg/L.** The collapse starts where a drain joins | Same |
| **52 of 347** sampled sites exceed the nitrate limit | CGWB |

**The single best line:**
> *"Nagpur has three million people. In the government's own 2023-24 report, twelve groundwater sources were sampled inside the city. Twelve. All twelve failed the bacteriological standard."*

## 3. WHO THIS IS FOR — and what each person actually gets

Be specific when asked "who uses this." Vague answers lose.

### The household on a borewell
Peri-urban and district households with no treatment at all. **Gets:** whether the nearest tested groundwater exceeded anything, and what to do about it. **Concretely:** if it's nitrate, we tell them boiling makes it *worse* — and that the risk is to bottle-fed infants under six months.

### The farmer
**Gets:** whether the water is fit for irrigation under CPCB Class E, and what it costs a specific crop under FAO standards. Orange loses ~91% yield at the worst site we found; cotton loses nothing on the same water. **56 of 347 sites would cost an orange grower more than 10% yield. Zero would harm cotton.**

### The city resident on municipal supply
**Gets — honestly — mostly "we don't know about your tap."** And that is the point, not a failure. What they do get: what's been measured near them, how far away it is, and the fact that a city-wide 99.6% cannot answer their question.

**If asked "so it's useless for most people?"** — see Q6 below. It has a good answer.

### The person about to spend money
Drilling a borewell (₹1.5 lakh), buying an RO (₹18,000), renting a flat. **Gets:** what's known about water in that area before the money is spent.

### The ward officer / municipal engineer
**Gets:** every agency's data on one screen. Currently it sits with a central board, a state board and a private operator, and no single one of them can produce this picture.

### The journalist, researcher, or activist
**Gets:** a citable, attributed, dated view of the city's water — with the empty areas visible.

## 4. WHAT'S BUILT vs WHAT'S NEXT

**Say this distinction out loud. It's what gets you believed on both halves.**

**Working now:**

## 5. THE QUESTIONS — and how to answer

### Q1 · "Doesn't this already exist?" *(in any form)*
> **"Yes. That's the point.** Almost everything we show already exists — in a PDF, in a department, in a report nobody opens. None of it reaches the person it's about. We're not a new measurement. We're the delivery layer."

**This one answer covers most versions of the question.** Say it early.

### Q2 · "Jal Jeevan Mission already publishes water quality."
> "It does — village-level results, published publicly, texted to residents. **But JJM is rural only.** A farmer outside Nagpur can look up his water. A resident inside Nagpur cannot. We're asking why the city limit is where that stops."

**Raise this yourself before a judge does.** It looks like research; being caught by it looks like you didn't check.

### Q3 · "Nagpur already has disaster management."
> "It does, and it works — but **all of it is response.** It activates when water is already in the street. **NMC's own Monsoon Preparedness Plan lists 66 low-lying flood-prone areas.** The city already knows. That list has never reached the people living in those 66 areas. Disaster management asks *how do we respond?* We ask *why did nobody tell them?*"

### Q4 · "The data is already public."
> "It's a 295-page PDF, a 203-page report, and 37 separate map files. **Public and reachable are different things — and that gap is the entire product.**"

### Q5 · "What's actually new?"
> "Three things. **Connection** — nobody has joined a central board, a state board and a municipal operator's data, because they belong to three different bodies. **Resolution** — turning 99.6% city-wide into an answer about your location. And **honesty** — we refuse to say your water is safe, because we don't have the data to say it."

### Q6 · "I know my water comes from NMC and it's filtered. Why do I need this?"
> "NMC treats it at the plant. Your RO treats it at your tap. **Neither covers what happens in between** — and published research on Nagpur found intermittent supply worsens microbial quality, because pipes that sit empty draw contamination in through leaks. Parts of this city get 18–24 hours of water; others get 2–4. **You have no way to know which you are.**
>
> And honestly — **if you're on 24-hour supply with a clean tank, you probably don't need this, and our app will tell you that.** It's for your neighbours on two hours a day, the households on borewells where all twelve sampled wells had faecal coliform, and the farmer downstream losing a third of his orange yield."

**Refusing to oversell here wins the exchange.** A judge asking this is testing whether you'll overclaim.

### Q7 · "It exceeds a limit. So what do I do?"
> "We tell you. And there's one thing almost nobody knows: **boiling fixes bacteria but makes nitrate worse** — it evaporates the water and concentrates the nitrate. **52 of our 347 sampled sites exceed the nitrate limit.** Boiling is the first thing every household does, and for those places it's the wrong move."

### Q8 · "Your nearest measurement is 8 km away — that's meaningless."
> "Agreed — **and we show you the 8 km instead of hiding it.** Most products would interpolate and hand you a confident colour. We tell you the nearest real test is 8 km away, because that fact *is* the finding."

### Q9 · "Does this make the city look bad?"
> "It shouldn't. **Nagpur tripled its sewage treatment capacity since 2011** — that's in our material. OCW's own number is 99.6% fit. Our argument isn't that anyone is failing. **The gap is resolution, not effort.**"

### Q10 · "Who pays for it after this?"
> "Almost nothing to run — public static data, no field hardware. Honestly, **this shouldn't be a startup. It should be a published feed.** If the utility starts publishing per-zone results and we become redundant, we've won."

### Q11 · "How do you know your data is right?"
> "We extracted it from the source PDFs and **checked it against the reports' own published statistics.** CGWB says 35 of 263 shallow samples exceed nitrate — our extraction returns exactly 35 of 263. Every headline figure reproduces."

### Q12 · "Was the sampling seasonal? Coliform spikes post-monsoon."
> **"We don't know, and it matters."** The sampling dates aren't in the tables we extracted, and coliform is seasonally sensitive. So we report it as what that campaign measured and we don't extrapolate.

**Answering "we don't know" to a methodology question is stronger than guessing.** They're testing whether you'll bluff.

## 6. THINGS TO NEVER SAY

- ❌ "Our app tells you if your water is **safe**" — we never say safe, and that's a design decision we're proud of
- ❌ "**First**" / "**only**" / "nobody has ever" — unnecessary and attackable
- ❌ "**All** wells in Nagpur are contaminated" — say *"all twelve sampled"*
- ❌ "**Deep borewells are worse**" — the data suggests it but isn't statistically significant
- ❌ "**265 MLD flows untreated**" — that's a 2011 figure. Current: ~520 generated, ~345 treated, ~175 untreated
- ❌ Anything implying the utility is negligent

And if there's a moment for an ask:

If a question comes that isn't on this sheet, answer it from that sentence. It generalises.
